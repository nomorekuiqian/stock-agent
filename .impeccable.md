## Design Context

### Users
Chinese retail investors analyzing the US stock market. They range from beginners who don't know financial jargon to semi-experienced individuals tracking a watchlist. They use the app on both desktop and mobile equally, often during market hours or evenings when reviewing the day's activity. Desktop users do deep research (earnings analysis, multi-dimensional stock study). Mobile users do quick checks (price, key metrics, latest news). Both experiences must be excellent, but they can show different information density.

### Brand Personality
Professional, warm, understated. Three words: **calm, intelligent, trustworthy**. Not a flashy fintech app. Not a Bloomberg terminal. Think: a knowledgeable friend who happens to have access to institutional-grade data, speaking in plain Chinese.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Theme**: Dark-first ("Financial Terminal meets Editorial Luxury"). Near-black backgrounds (#050506), emerald accent (#10b981/#059669).
- **Typography**: LXGW WenKai (Chinese editorial headings), Instrument Serif (English display), HarmonyOS Sans SC + DM Sans (body), JetBrains Mono (data/numbers). Custom utility classes: `.font-editorial`, `.font-data`.
- **Anti-references**: Generic SaaS dashboards (too corporate), Robinhood (too gamified). Bloomberg Terminal is too dense for general pages but the stock research/earnings pages should lean toward higher density. The ChatGPT/Claude chat experience is the closest reference for the Agent UI, specifically the clean conversational flow with a side workspace.
- **Information density by page**: Daily reports and for-you pages stay editorial and calm. Stock research and earnings pages are denser, filling available screen space with charts and tables side by side. No excessive whitespace on desktop for data pages.
- **Color palette**: Emerald primary, near-black surfaces, zinc grays for borders/muted text. No purple, no gradient text, no neon.

### Design Principles
1. **Simplicity over capability showcase**: Never expose tool names, technical taxonomies, or implementation details to users. Progressive disclosure: let users discover capabilities through conversation.
2. **Data speaks, chrome doesn't**: Charts, numbers, and analysis should be the visual stars. UI chrome (borders, containers, navigation) should recede.
3. **Calm confidence**: No flashy animations, no exclamation marks, no urgency tactics. The interface should feel like sitting down with a thoughtful analyst.
4. **Conversational, not transactional**: The Agent is a conversation partner, not a form or a dashboard. The chat should flow naturally; the right panel provides visual context without demanding attention.
5. **Chinese-first**: All user-facing copy in Chinese. Financial jargon translated to plain language. Cultural sensitivity in tone (warm but not overly casual).
6. **Charts + Tables = Complete**: Financial data pages use both Recharts charts (for trends and visual comparison) and structured tables (for precise numbers). Neither alone is sufficient. Charts show the story, tables show the facts.
7. **Adaptive density**: Desktop data pages (stock research, earnings) fill the screen with `max-w-[1400px]+`. Content pages (daily reports) stay at `max-w-4xl`. Mobile collapses gracefully to single-column with key metrics first.

### Typography & Readability Rules (CRITICAL)
See `docs/constraints/design-system.md` for full spec. Key rules enforced across all Impeccable skills:
- **Chinese body text minimum 14px** (`text-sm`). 12px (`text-xs`) only for EN labels/timestamps.
- **NO `text-[Npx]` arbitrary values** (SVG exception only). Use the 9-level type scale.
- **NO `text-muted-foreground/60` or below** — contrast too low. Minimum `/70`, prefer no opacity.
- **NO `leading-none` on text below 24px** — kills readability for Chinese characters.
- **3 weight tiers only**: 400 (body) / 500 (labels) / 600 (headings). No `font-bold` except Hero.
- **`.font-data` for numbers only** — never for prose, labels, or descriptions.
